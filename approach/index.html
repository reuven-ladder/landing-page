Our approach

AI-native cost engineering. More analysis behind every cost decision.

A construction estimate depends on how well the project is understood. Ladder uses AI to connect drawings, specifications and revisions, giving cost professionals a structured basis to examine the scope, quantities and pricing.

For your team, that means a clearer view of what the project will cost, and the decisions that can change it.

01 / The challenge

More information. The same deadline.

A project’s requirements are spread across drawings, specifications, schedules and revisions.

Understanding the work means bringing those pieces together: what must be built, how much is required and which conditions affect the cost.

That work takes time. The deadline rarely makes room for all of it.

02 / What needs examination

Every estimate starts with assumptions.

An early budget. A familiar assembly. A rate from a previous project. Experience gives these starting points value. The next step is examining whether they fit this project.

What has changed? What does the design require? What is still unknown?

We make those questions part of the analysis, with assumptions clearly stated for review.

03 / Where AI fits

Connect the project before pricing the work.

Our AI organizes requirements by trade, connects drawing and specification references, extracts quantities and flags conflicting information.

That brings the information an estimate needs into a form cost professionals can examine together.

Scope, quantities and assumptions stay connected as the estimate is developed, reviewed and revised.

04 / How we work

Specialist AI agents. A cost engineer leading the work.

Our AI agents examine different parts of the project in parallel: connecting requirements, checking quantities and comparing information across documents.

A cost engineer brings those findings together, challenges assumptions and directs further checks where needed. The analysis feeds the review. The review shapes the next round of analysis.

You receive a coordinated estimate, with its scope, pricing basis and unresolved questions clearly stated.

Questions

Before you ask for a quote.

How is AI used?
Project information is organized for analysis from the start: requirements by trade, references connected across documents, quantities extracted and conflicts flagged. Cost professionals review that analysis, and their decisions on pricing, allowances and uncertainty become part of the estimate.
Can the AI get it wrong?
Yes. It can misread a sheet or anchor on the wrong reference, the same way a person can. That is why quantities carry their source references and a cost professional reviews the items that move the number. We describe what the analysis covered rather than promise it found everything.
What happens when the documents don’t answer a question?
It gets one of four treatments: a clarification for the design team, a stated assumption, an allowance, or an exclusion. Each is listed with the estimate. Some need the designer’s answer before they can close.
Can you update the estimate when the design changes?
Yes. Because scope, quantities and assumptions stay connected to their sources, a revision can be assessed against the existing cost basis rather than estimated from scratch.
Who reviews the estimate?
An experienced cost professional, named in your proposal with their experience and scope of review.
Do I need to send drawings to get a quote?
No. Describe the project first. We ask for documents once the scope is clear. NDA on request.
How long, and what does it cost?
A fixed fee. We confirm the scope, fee and delivery schedule with you before starting.

Validation

AI-only backtests on public bids.

We run the AI analysis alone on public bid documents, without seeing the results, and compare it with the award. These runs have no professional review, and an award reflects the winning bidder’s own scope and pricing, so the difference is a measure of the raw analysis, not of a delivered estimate.
