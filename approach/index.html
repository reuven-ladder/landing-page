Why Ladder

Estimating, rebuilt around AI.

A look behind the scenes: how a construction estimate gets made today, and how we make it.

Ladder is a cost estimating firm. Not an AI estimate: an estimate made by cost engineers, with AI doing the reading.

See behind the scenes ↓ View a sample estimate

01 / The job

One number. A dozen sources.

An estimator reconciles drawings, specs, addenda, sub quotes, the last job’s numbers and their own experience into a single price.

And when the spec says “see drawings” and the drawings say “see spec”, someone has to chase it down.

02 / The work

No time to read it all. No time to ask.

Even with digital takeoff, clicking every door and wall and re-keying it into a spreadsheet eats the clock. Few estimators ever get far enough ahead to read the whole set and send their questions before the cutoff.

So questions turn into assumptions, and assumptions into risk.

03 / The pressure

Tight deadlines. Thin margins.

Addenda keep arriving and the due date doesn’t move. A missed item comes straight out of margin, and one bad estimate can wipe out a year’s profit.

Worse, the low bid often wins because something was missed.

04 / The lost lesson

And nothing comes back.

Win or lose, the job gets a folder that’s never deleted and rarely opened again. What the team learned about this set, this trade or this sub seldom reaches the next estimate.

Every bid starts almost from scratch.

05 / The human limit

Even the best estimator is human.

Under deadline, judgment takes shortcuts in predictable ways:

  • FatiguePage 900 doesn’t get the attention page 9 did. A set with a “ground floor” and a “first floor” loses one of them.
  • AnchoringA number that looks like last time goes in without a second look.
  • OptimismEstimates drift toward what the project hopes to spend.

None of it is carelessness. It’s what deadlines do to people.

06 / Why now

We built an estimating department, staffed by AI.

AI can now read a full set and cross-check it: page against page, trade against trade. That’s the part estimators never have time for. So we built a department of specialist agents, one per trade, that read every page with the same attention.

  1. 1Trade agents read the whole set.
  2. 2Conflicts surface with their sources.

It reads, counts and cross-checks. We call it the AI cost engineer.

07 / Human in the loop

Not bulletproof. So an expert steers it.

AI that makes things up is the industry’s biggest fear, and it should be. So every quantity links to the sheet it came from, and nothing reaches the estimate until our cost engineer has checked it.

  1. 3The engineer challenges and directs.
  2. 4The decision goes into the estimate.

The grunt work goes. The judgment stays.

08 / AI-native

Every project makes the next one sharper.

Every quantity, question and decision is logged with its source. What we learn on one project becomes a check on the next.

It’s the feedback loop bidding never had.
