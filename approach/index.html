Why Ladder

Estimating, rebuilt around AI.

A live estimate built on real data changes a project. It can steer the design, control spending and catch errors and omissions while they’re still cheap to fix. Today that estimate depends on scarce hours from experts.

01 / When cost is decided

Most of the cost is set before anyone prices it in detail.

The early choices decide the cost: the massing, the structural system, the envelope, the scope. Each one is cheap to change at concept and expensive once the drawings are done.

By the end of construction documents, the main cost lever left is downgrading finishes.

02 / How estimates are made today

The earliest estimates rest on the least.

At concept, the information is thin, the deadline is short and the estimate gets the least time. So the number comes from an experienced person, often a cost per square foot from a similar past job, adjusted by judgment.

Detailed estimates arrive later, at milestones, after weeks of manual takeoff. By then many of the decisions have already been made.

03 / The part nobody budgets for

Experience comes with bias.

It isn’t carelessness. Research on project forecasts shows that people misjudge cost in predictable ways:

  • AnchoringThe first number, or the last project’s rate, pulls every number after it.
  • OptimismEstimates drift toward what the project hopes to spend.
  • Blind spotsPages nobody had time to read never reach the number.

These shortcuts get worse under deadline pressure, and a single total hides them.

04 / Our approach

We built an estimating department, staffed by AI.

A lead splits the project into trade packages, the way a chief estimator would. Each trade gets its own specialist agent that reads every sheet and spec section in its slice.

A senior reviewer checks each package against what that trade usually carries, and writes findings without changing the numbers. Neighboring trades are then reconciled pair by pair, because scope gaps hide at the boundaries. Anything still unresolved becomes an RFI.

Our cost engineer leads the whole department.

05 / Rules a deadline can’t bend

Discipline people keep when they can, built into every estimate.

  • Every spec article is used or dropped with a stated reason. None goes unread.
  • Every quantity says where it came from: printed, calculated, measured or judgment.
  • One measurement gives one number, in every trade that uses it.
  • Every package comes with marked-up drawings that show its evidence.

06 / Experience that compounds

Every project makes the next one sharper.

The best-documented cure for forecasting bias is to look outside the project: at what comparable work actually took and cost.

We carry trade knowledge from every project we’ve estimated: what each trade usually includes, where scope tends to fall between trades and which questions a set like yours usually raises.

An anchor has to earn its place.

07 / People who own the number

Our cost engineers sign it.

The agents do the reading. Our cost professionals do the judgment: they direct the work, challenge the findings, weigh alternatives and decide how each open question is handled.
